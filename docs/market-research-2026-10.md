# Market and search research, October 2026

Done on 17 October 2026 for the copy and page rewrite. Two research passes, one on Phoenix competitors and one on search. The search tool used was not Google itself, so "what ranks" below means what came up for the query, not a true Google results page. No free search volume numbers were found for these rental terms, so none are given.

## What the Phoenix competitors look like

The sites that read as a real business show an address, an owner's first name, a Google review count, named venues or clients, and the year they started. Centric Events (centric.events) does this best: 600+ events, 147 Google reviews at 5.0, named clients like Carvana and MLB Spring Training. Xperience Entertainment (readytoxperience.com) names the Arizona Biltmore and The Phoenician and says it was founded in 2006. C West Entertainment (djcwest.com) names its owner and says "Since 2014".

Most of the copy is salesy ("elevate", "premier", "unforgettable"). Valley Video (valleyvideo.com/sound-system-rentals) is the plainest, and its H1 is just "Sound System and Speaker Rentals in Phoenix and the East Valley".

The stronger sites name their gear brands: C West (L-Acoustics, QSC KLA12, JBL SRX828SP), Arizona Stage (QSC KLA12, JBL VTX A8), ActivSound (RCF, CDJ-3000, DJM-A9). Bros Entertainment in Tucson lists QSC LA112, the same tops we run.

Very few publish prices. The ones found:

- pasoundsystemrental.com: DJ packages from $225 (under 200 people) to $1,200 (four QSC line array boxes on two 18" subs). Delivery, setup and a technician are extra.
- djcwest.com: two speakers on stands $400 plus $150 delivery. An L-Acoustics system for 500 to 1,000 people "starts between $4,500 and $9,500".
- Premier Denver Event Rentals: two BASSBOSS DV12 tops and two ZV28 subs for $550 plus travel.

Nobody in Phoenix publishes an all-in price for a full system with an engineer.

## Gaps nobody in Arizona covers

- Battery power. No Arizona site found mentions it. Centric and Xperience talk about generators.
- Electronic music. C West lists "EDM events" once in a list. Nobody names BASSBOSS, Funktion-One or any sub brand built for it, and nobody has a page for club nights, warehouse parties or raves. ActivSound (nightclub roots, pool parties) is the closest.
- Desert and outdoor events. Only an Xperience blog post touches them.
- Crowd size guidance. Rare. pasoundsystemrental.com and C West are the only ones that give headcounts.

Outside Arizona, EDM-focused rental companies put the brand in the page title (Lost Horizon in Denver: "Funktion-One Rental Colorado"; Premier Denver: "Rave Sound System, Funktion One, BASSBOSS"). We don't name gear on our site, by choice, so this is noted and not used.

## Search terms

Worth targeting, most crowded first:

- "sound system rental phoenix" / "PA system rental phoenix". Service pages rank, not home pages (Centric, The Penn Group, Arizona Stage).
- "dj sound system rental phoenix". Rent For Event, Centric, and Thumbtack's Phoenix DJ pages.
- "line array rental phoenix". Only one local result (The Penn Group, which is an Ohio company with templated city pages). Few competitors.
- "speaker rental phoenix", "audio equipment rental scottsdale". City pages rank for the Scottsdale query (Hartford Rents, Penn).

Not worth a page:

- "pa rental phoenix" returns apartments in Phoenixville, Pennsylvania. Always write "PA system".
- "pool party sound system scottsdale" returns venues and party listings, not rental companies.
- "battery powered sound system rental" mostly returns single portable speakers. Our battery page says in its first paragraph that it's the full system.
- "sound engineer for hire phoenix" returns studio mixing freelancers.

## What Google's own documentation says

- The meta keywords tag isn't used by Google at all (developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/special-tags). Removed from the site.
- FAQ rich results stopped showing in Google Search on 7 May 2026 (developers.google.com/search/updates). The FAQ JSON-LD is still on the home page; it costs nothing and other search engines may read it.
- A service-area business should hide its street address in its Business Profile, can list up to 20 service areas, and the area shouldn't be more than about two hours' drive from the base (support.google.com/business/answer/3038177 and /answer/9157481).
- Local ranking comes from relevance, distance and prominence, and reviews feed prominence (support.google.com/business/answer/7091).
- Pages aimed at cities that only funnel visitors to one page count as doorway pages, which is spam (developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). That's why there are no city pages yet.

## Listings

The Google Business Profile matters most, since local results and Maps draw on it. After that: Bing Places (it can import the Google profile), Apple Business Connect (a service-area business may not get a public place card; check during signup), Thumbtack (its Phoenix AV rentals page had one new provider with no reviews), GigSalad (SoundMoverz in Scottsdale is there with an L-Acoustics rig), Giggster and Eventective.

## What was built from this

- Three service pages: /line-array-rental, /dj-sound-system-rental, /battery-powered-sound, each with its own title, description, Service and breadcrumb structured data, and the quote form.
- Home page H1 is now "Sound system rental in Phoenix, Arizona".
- Meta keywords removed. Sitemap lists the new pages.

## What would help most and can only come from Daniel

- A Google Business Profile, with the address hidden and the metro cities as service areas, and reviews from past clients on it.
- Names of venues or events the system has played, with permission, and the photos from them. This is the biggest thing separating the real-looking competitor sites from ours.
- An owner name and a starting year for an About section.
- Whether to publish a starting price.
- Whether to name the gear. Nobody in Arizona names BASSBOSS, and EDM rental companies elsewhere put their brands in their page titles.
- A Scottsdale or Tucson page, but only with real content behind it, like past events there.
